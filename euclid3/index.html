Review: Vertical Angles Theorem

When two lines intersect, the vertical angles formed are congruent (equal in measure).

We proved this theorem in Lesson II using the Linear Pair Postulate. We will use it again today.

Parallel Lines

Parallel lines are lines in the same plane that never intersect, no matter how far they are extended.

We write ℓ ∥ m to indicate that line ℓ is parallel to line m.

Parallel lines are often marked with matching arrows.

The Transversal

A transversal is a line that intersects two or more other lines at distinct points.

When a transversal t crosses two parallel lines ℓ and m, it creates eight angles. We label them a through h as shown.

Corresponding Angles

Corresponding angles are angles that are in the same position at each intersection.

Examples from the diagram:

  • ∠a and ∠e are corresponding (both upper-left)
  • ∠b and ∠f are corresponding (both upper-right)
  • ∠c and ∠g are corresponding (both lower-right)
  • ∠d and ∠h are corresponding (both lower-left)

Corresponding Angles Postulate

If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, then corresponding angles are congruent.

If ℓ ∥ m, then: ∠a = ∠e, ∠b = ∠f, ∠c = ∠g, ∠d = ∠h

This is a postulate—we accept it as true without proof. It will be our tool for proving the theorems that follow.

Alternate Interior Angles

Alternate interior angles are angles that are:

  1. 1. On opposite sides of the transversal
  2. 2. Between the parallel lines (interior)
  3. 3. At different intersections

Examples from the diagram:

  • ∠c and ∠e are alternate interior angles
  • ∠d and ∠f are alternate interior angles

They are called "alternate" because they are on alternating sides of the transversal.

Theorem: Alternate Interior Angles

Alternate Interior Angles Theorem

If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, then alternate interior angles are congruent.

Given: ℓ ∥ m, cut by transversal t
Prove: ∠c = ∠e
Statement
Reason
1. ℓ ∥ m
Given
2. ∠a = ∠e
Corresponding Angles Postulate
3. ∠a = ∠c
Vertical Angles Theorem
4. ∠c = ∠e
Transitive Property of Equality
(if x = y and y = z, then x = z)
∎

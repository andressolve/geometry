Two Lines, One Grid

Two lines. Different slopes — so they're not parallel. They cross somewhere. Where exactly?

Find the Crossing Point

Click on the point where the two lines cross.

(2, 4). That's where the two lines meet.

Why That Point?

Is (2, 4) on y = 2x?

y = 2 × 2 = 4 ✓

Is (2, 4) on y = x + 2?

y = 2 + 2 = 4 ✓

(2, 4) is the only point that lies on both lines. That's what makes it the crossing point.

The point where two lines cross is called their intersection. It's the one point whose coordinates satisfy both equations.

Where Lines Meet

Lines with different slopes cross at exactly one point.

That point is called the intersection.

The intersection is the one point whose coordinates satisfy both equations.

You can find it by reading the graph, or by comparing tables of values.

Different slopes → one crossing point → the intersection.

The Knight Walks the Line

Remember the knight? Start at the origin. Every move is the same: right 1, up 2.

Click Step and watch where the knight lands.

The same move, over and over, makes a line.

That up 2 in the move has a name: the slope. It tells you how fast the line climbs.

Two Points of View

Every line can be written y = mx + b.

m is the slope — the knight's up-step.
b is where the knight starts, on the y-axis.

Same climb, different start

The line y = 2x + 3 climbs just like y = 2x — right 1, up 2 — but the knight begins at (0, 3).

What if the knight goes down?

For y = −x + 5, each move is right 1, down 1. The line falls. Its slope is −1.

A positive slope climbs. A negative slope falls. Either way, the slope is the number in front of x.

Same Slope, Parallel

These two lines have the same slope — both climb right 1, up 1. One just starts higher than the other.

No matter how far you extend them, they never touch.

Lines that never meet are called parallel. Two lines are parallel when they have the same slope.

Different slopes cross

If the slopes are different, one line is steeper. Sooner or later it catches the other, and they cross.

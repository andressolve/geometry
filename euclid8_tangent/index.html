Tangent Lines

When does a line touch a circle just once?

Lesson VIII

Scroll to begin

What is a Tangent Line?

A tangent line is a line that touches a circle at exactly one point.

Like a wheel rolling on the ground—the ground is tangent to the wheel.

The point where they touch is called the point of tangency.

Two Ways to Meet

A line and circle can meet in two ways:

Tangent line

Touches at exactly one point

Secant line

Crosses through at two points

Looking at Secants

When a line crosses a circle at two points, call them A and B.

Connect both points to the center O.

This makes triangle OAB.

Remember: OA = OB because they're both radii.

So triangle OAB is isosceles!

Remember isosceles triangles from Lesson 6?

A Perpendicular Line

Now let's look at something special.

Draw a radius OP (from center O to point P on the circle).

Draw a line perpendicular to OP at point P.

This makes a right angle at P.

Question: Could this line cross the circle at another point?

Let's investigate...

Let's Suppose It Does

Let's suppose the perpendicular line crosses the circle at another point.

Call that second point Q.

Now we have two points on the circle: P and Q.

Connect O to Q to form triangle OPQ.

Since OP = OQ (both radii), triangle OPQ is isosceles.

So the angles at P and Q must be equal.

We're assuming this is possible. Let's see what happens...

Exercise

We know the line is perpendicular to OP at point P.

So the angle at P = 90°

Triangle OPQ is isosceles with OP = OQ.

What is the angle at Q?

Something's Wrong!

Let's add up the angles in triangle OPQ:

  • Angle at P = 90°
  • Angle at Q = 90°
  • Angle at O = some positive amount (it can't be 0°)

Total = 90° + 90° + (angle at O)

That's more than 180°!

But wait... we learned that angles in a triangle always add up to exactly 180°.

This can't happen!

So what went wrong?

• We started by supposing the line crossed the circle at two points: P and Q

• That assumption led us to an impossible situation

• When an assumption leads to something impossible, the assumption must be wrong

• The line cannot cross the circle at two points

• It can only touch the circle at one point: P

Our assumption was wrong. A line perpendicular to a radius can only touch the circle at one point!

The Proof

Why a Perpendicular Line is Tangent

Given: Circle with center O, radius OP, and a line perpendicular to OP at point P.
Claim: The line touches the circle only at point P.

Proof: Suppose the line crosses the circle at another point Q (in addition to P). Then OP and OQ are both radii, so OP = OQ. This means triangle OPQ is isosceles.

In an isosceles triangle, the base angles are equal. Since the line is perpendicular to OP at point P, the angle at P is 90°. Because the base angles are equal, the angle at Q must also be 90°.

Now let's add up the angles in triangle OPQ. We have 90° at P, 90° at Q, and some positive angle at O. The sum is 90° + 90° + (angle at O), which is more than 180°.

But we know that the angles in any triangle always sum to exactly 180°. This is a contradiction!

Our assumption must be wrong. The line cannot cross the circle at a second point Q. So the line touches the circle at exactly one point: P.

A line that touches a circle at exactly one point is called a tangent line. We've just proved that a line perpendicular to a radius at its endpoint is tangent to the circle.

∎

The Theorem

What we just proved:

A line perpendicular to a radius at its endpoint is tangent to the circle.

Or in other words: A line perpendicular to a radius touches the circle at exactly one point.

This is why when you draw a tangent line to a circle, it always looks perpendicular to the radius at that point!

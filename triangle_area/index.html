Area of a Triangle

For Sebastian — a small, careful walkthrough.

Every triangle is half of a rectangle. Let's see why.

Half a Rectangle

Here is a rectangle. It is 6 wide and 4 tall.

A diagonal line cuts it into two equal triangles.

The rectangle's area is 6 × 4 = 24.

The two triangles are the same size. So each triangle has area:

24 ÷ 2 = 12

One triangle = half the rectangle.

Any Triangle

What about a triangle that isn't a right triangle? Like this one:

We can still wrap it in a rectangle. The base is 8, the height is 5.

Drop a vertical line from the top point down to the base. This splits the triangle into two right triangles.

Look at each half:

  • The left right triangle is half of the left rectangle.
  • The right right triangle is half of the right rectangle.

So the whole triangle is half of the whole rectangle.

Area = (base × height) ÷ 2
= (8 × 5) ÷ 2 = 40 ÷ 2 = 20

The Formula

Area of a triangle  =  (base × height) ÷ 2

Three things to remember:

  1. Base — pick any side. Usually the bottom one.
  2. Height — the straight-up distance from the base to the opposite point. Always perpendicular to the base.
  3. Multiply, then divide by 2. Always divide by 2.

The dashed line shows the height. It meets the base at a right angle.

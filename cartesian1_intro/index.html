One Number Isn't Enough

On a number line, one number tells you exactly where a point is.

But on a flat surface, you need more information.

How do we describe where a point is in two dimensions?

Two Axes, One Plane

Take two number lines.

Place one horizontally — we call it the x-axis.

Place one vertically — we call it the y-axis.

They cross at zero. Together, they form the Cartesian plane.

The Origin

The point where the axes cross is called the origin.

It's the starting point — the center of the coordinate system.

The origin is at (0, 0)

Every other point is described by how far it is from here.

Coordinates

To locate a point, we measure:

  1. How far along the x-axis (left or right)
  2. How far along the y-axis (up or down)

Coordinates: An ordered pair (x, y) that describes a point's position.

x comes first, then y.

Reading Points

To read a point's coordinates:

  1. Look at how far it is along the x-axis (horizontal)
  2. Look at how far it is along the y-axis (vertical)

Important: The order matters!

(3, 4) and (4, 3) are different points.

What You Learned

The Cartesian plane is formed by two perpendicular number lines.

The x-axis is horizontal. The y-axis is vertical.

They meet at the origin: (0, 0).

Every point has coordinates written as (x, y) — x first, then y.

A Point on Two Lines

Here are two lines:

y = x + 1
y = 2x

They cross somewhere. Click on the crossing point.

 

The crossing point is (1, 2).

Let's check. Does (1, 2) pass both tests?

y = x + 1  →  1 + 1 = 2   ✓

y = 2x  →  2 × 1 = 2   ✓

Both equations give y = 2 when x = 1.

That's what makes it the crossing point — it's on both lines at once.

Same y

At the crossing point, both lines have the same y.

The first line says:

y = x + 1

The second line says:

y = 2x

If they give the same y, then the two expressions must be equal:

x + 1 = 2x

This is one equation with one unknown.

We can solve it — no graph needed.

Step by Step

Let's solve x + 1 = 2x one step at a time.

x + 1 = 2x
Subtract x from both sides:
x + 1 − x = 2x − x
On the left, x − x is nothing. On the right, 2x − x is x.
1 = x
Now we know x = 1. Plug it into either equation to find y:
y = 2x = 2(1) = 2
The crossing point is:
(1, 2)

Two Lines, One Point

Two different lines (with different slopes) cross at exactly one point.

At that point, both equations give the same y. Set the two expressions equal, and you get one equation with one unknown.

Solve for x. Then plug back in to find y.

No graph needed — the algebra finds the exact crossing point.

To find where two lines cross: set the y-expressions equal, solve for x, then find y.

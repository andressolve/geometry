# Lesson 7: Angles in Circles - Specification

## Lesson Overview
A discovery-based lesson where students explore circles and angles through exercises. They calculate, notice patterns, and prove what they find.

**Approach:** Foundation → Exercises → Notice pattern → Explore interactively → Prove it

### Page 1: What is a Circle?
**Content:**
- A circle is all points that are the same distance from a center point
- This distance is called the **radius**
- Every point on the circle is exactly one radius away from the center

**Diagram:** Circle with center O, several radii drawn to different points, all marked equal with tick marks.

---

### Page 2: Triangles from the Center
**Content:**
- Pick any two points on the circle: A and B
- Connect them to the center O
- Triangle OAB has two sides that are radii
- Since all radii are equal: OA = OB → **isosceles triangle!**

**Diagram:** Circle with center O, points A and B on circle, triangle OAB with tick marks showing OA = OB.

**Connection:** "Remember isosceles triangles from Lesson 6?"

---

### Page 3: Exercise 0
**Exercise (image0):**
- Two points on the circle looking at the same arc
- One angle is 20°
- What's the other angle?

**Diagram:** Circle with two angles at different points on the circle, both looking at the same arc. One labeled 20°, other labeled "?".

**Answer:** 20°

**Discovery:** Angles on the circle looking at the same arc are equal!

### Page 4: Exercise 1
**Exercise (image1):**
- Point C on the circle, looking at arc AB
- The angle at C is split into two parts: 25° and 5°
- Total angle at C = 30°
- Lines drawn to the center O
- What is the angle at O?

**Diagram:** Circle with angle of 30° at C (shown as 25° + 5°), radii drawn to A and B. Angle at O = "?".

**Answer:** 60°

**Observation:** Hmm, 60° is double 30°...

---

### Page 5: Exercise 2
**Exercise (image2):**
- Same setup, but angle at C split differently: 10° and 20°
- Total angle at C = 30°
- What is the angle at O?

**Diagram:** Circle with angle of 30° at C (shown as 10° + 20°), angle at O = "?".

**Answer:** 60°

**Observation:** Same answer! No matter how we split 30°, the angle at the center is 60°.

### Page 6: See It In Action (Interactive)
**Interactive tool:**
- Circle with center O
- Points A and B on circle (angle at O is 60°)
- Point C can be dragged anywhere on the arc
- Display shows the angle at C in real-time
- As C moves: angle at C stays at 30°!

**What students see:**
- No matter where C is on the arc, angle at C = 30°
- The angle at the center is always double the angle on the circle
- This confirms the pattern from the exercises

**Controls:** Drag point C around the arc.

---

### Page 7: Why Does This Work?
**Content:**
- Let's prove this for our 30° case
- We'll use what we know about isosceles triangles

**Setup diagram:** Circle with center O, points A, B, C on circle. C at top, A and B at bottom. Angle at C = 30°. Radii OA, OB, OC drawn.

---

### Page 8: The Proof

**Given:** Circle with center O. Points A, B, C on the circle. Angle ACB = 30°.

**Prove:** Angle AOB = 60°

| Statement | Reason |
|-----------|--------|
| 1. OA = OC | Radii of a circle are equal |
| 2. OB = OC | Radii of a circle are equal |
| 3. Triangle OAC is isosceles | OA = OC (statement 1) |
| 4. Triangle OBC is isosceles | OB = OC (statement 2) |
| 5. Let angle OCA = α, angle OCB = β | — |
| 6. α + β = 30° | Given (angle ACB = 30°) |
| 7. Angle AOC = 2α | Exterior angle of isosceles triangle = 2 × base angle |
| 8. Angle BOC = 2β | Exterior angle of isosceles triangle = 2 × base angle |
| 9. Angle AOB = 2α + 2β | Angle AOB = angle AOC + angle BOC |
| 10. Angle AOB = 2(α + β) = 2 × 30° = 60° | Substitution from statement 6 |

∎

---

### Page 9: The Pattern
**What we proved:**
- When the angle at C is 30°, the angle at O is 60°
- The angle at the center is **twice** the angle on the circle (looking at the same arc)

This works for any angle, not just 30°!

---

### Page 10: What You Discovered
**Summary:**
- All radii of a circle are equal
- This creates isosceles triangles when you connect points to the center
- Angles on the circle looking at the same arc are equal
- The angle at the center = 2 × the angle on the circle
